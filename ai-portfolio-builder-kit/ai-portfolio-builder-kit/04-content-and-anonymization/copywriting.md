# Copywriting

The portfolio's voice. Short guidance, high leverage.

## Principles

- **Lead with the result.** The first sentence of any page or section should answer "what happened" or "what is this." Supporting detail comes after.
- **Write in complete sentences, but keep them tight.** Readable beats terse. Don't compress into fragments and arrow-chains; do cut whole sentences that don't earn their place.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Cut onboarding from hours of recordings to active queries" beats "improved onboarding efficiency."
- **One voice.** Decide warm-and-personal vs. crisp-and-corporate (from intake) and hold it across every page.
- **The reader scans.** Front-load. Use meaningful section headings. Assume seconds of attention, not minutes.

## Length targets (rough)

| Surface | Target |
|---|---|
| Home hero | 1 line + 1 supporting line |
| About bio | 2–4 short paragraphs |
| Case study section | 1–3 short paragraphs + a visual |
| Image caption | 1 sentence |
| Testimonial | 1–2 sentences |

## What to cut

- Any sentence that restates the previous one.
- Numbers already shown in a stats panel (connect them to the story instead).
- Generic filler: "passionate about crafting delightful experiences." Say something only you could say.
- Jargon that hides meaning. If a nine-year-old couldn't get the gist, simplify.

## Bilingual copy

- Don't machine-translate voice blindly; the tone is the point. Review both languages.
- Keep both languages in sync as content changes.
- Translate the *whole* experience: alt text, captions, ARIA labels, metadata — not just body text.

## Privacy in copy

Anonymize as you write, never as a cleanup pass. The moment you type a real client name into a draft, it's one careless commit away from shipping. Use the agreed alias from the first keystroke.
